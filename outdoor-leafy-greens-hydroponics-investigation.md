# DIY Outdoor Hydroponic System for Leafy Greens

Date: 2026-05-09

## Recommendation

Build a small aerated deep-water culture system for the first version. For DIY leafy greens, DWC is more forgiving than NFT because roots have more water volume, brief pump interruptions are less catastrophic, and construction is simpler.

For food production, use food-contact PP or HDPE reservoirs and wetted parts.

The main outdoor risk is not plumbing complexity. It is hot nutrient solution, which reduces dissolved oxygen and increases root disease risk. Treat water temperature control as a core design requirement.

## Gemini Verifier Summary

Gemini was used as an opposing verifier. It agreed with the basic DWC direction but challenged several assumptions:

- Shade cloth and an opaque reservoir are not enough if the water gets too warm.
- Municipal water can contain chloramine and high alkalinity, which can cause pH drift.
- Weekly checks may be too infrequent during hot weather because evaporation can raise EC quickly.
- Mixed-crop reservoirs can be inefficient because lettuce, spinach, chard, and brassicas do not all prefer the same EC.
- Small light leaks around net cups or lid holes can cause algae.
- Outdoor systems need pest exclusion, not just nutrient management.

The most important design change from the verifier critique is to add a real water-temperature strategy: shade, insulation, partial burial, or another way to keep the reservoir cool.

## Preferred System

Use:

- Food-safe 5-gallon HDPE bucket modules, or an NSF food-service PP/HDPE reservoir
- Food-contact reservoir lid
- 2-inch net cups
- 1 full-size plant per 5-gallon bucket, or 4-6 plant sites in a compact food-service reservoir
- Aquarium air pump
- 2 air stones
- Optional small circulation pump
- Hydroponic leafy-greens nutrient
- pH meter or pH strips
- EC meter
- pH up/down labeled for food or hydroponic use
- 30-50% shade cloth for warm months
- Insect netting or row-cover fabric
- GFCI-protected outdoor power

Best placement:

- Put the reservoir in shade.
- In a greenhouse, use insulation, shade, partial burial, larger water volume, or another active cooling strategy before scaling.
- Keep all nutrient solution surfaces dark.
- Avoid placing the reservoir on hot concrete in full sun.

## Crops

Best first crops:

- Looseleaf lettuce
- Mini romaine
- Butterhead lettuce
- Bok choy or pak choi
- Kale
- Mustards
- Basil
- Cilantro
- Parsley

Do not mix too many crop types in the first reservoir. Start with lettuce plus one compatible crop such as bok choy or herbs. Spinach, chard, and heavier brassicas may benefit from a separate reservoir with higher EC.

## Operating Targets

For lettuce and beginner leafy greens:

- pH: 5.8-6.5
- EC: 1.2-1.8 mS/cm
- Nutrient solution temperature: keep below about 75 F when possible
- Air temperature: cool to mild is best; sustained 80 F+ weather increases bolting and bitterness

For heavier greens:

- Spinach and chard often prefer higher EC than lettuce.
- Bok choy and brassicas can tolerate moderately higher feeding.
- If growth is poor in a mixed reservoir, separate crops by nutrient demand.

## Water

Use potable municipal or filtered water. Do not use untreated rainwater for leafy greens because it can carry bacteria and parasites.

Before committing to a nutrient routine, test source water for:

- pH
- EC
- Alkalinity, if available

High alkalinity can make pH drift upward after adjustment. If that happens, test more frequently and consider nutrients formulated for hard water.

## Build Sequence

1. Drill net-cup holes in a food-contact bucket lid or food-service lid.
2. Install air stones at the bottom of the reservoir.
3. Fill the reservoir with potable water.
4. Add hydroponic nutrients until EC reaches the target range.
5. Adjust pH after nutrients are fully mixed.
6. Start seeds in rockwool, coco plugs, or another inert starter.
7. Transplant when seedlings have true leaves and visible roots.
8. Cover the crop area with insect netting.
9. Add shade cloth during strong sun or hot weather.
10. Harvest outer leaves continuously or harvest whole heads at maturity.

## Maintenance Schedule

Daily in hot weather:

- Check water level.
- Confirm the air pump is bubbling.
- Look for wilting, algae, pests, brown roots, or bad odor.

Two to three times per week:

- Check pH.
- Check EC.
- Top off with water first.
- Add nutrients only if EC remains low after topping off.

Every one to two weeks:

- Replace nutrient solution if pH or EC becomes unstable.
- Replace solution if water smells bad, algae builds up, roots brown, or the crop cycle is long.
- Clean debris and seal any light leaks.

Between crops:

- Wash reservoir, lid, net cups, and air stones.
- Remove old roots.
- Restart with fresh nutrient solution.

## Failure Modes and Mitigations

| Failure mode | Likely cause | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brown roots or slimy roots | Warm water, low oxygen, pathogen pressure | Shade or bury reservoir, increase aeration, clean system, replace solution |
| Algae growth | Light reaching nutrient solution | Use opaque reservoir, cover net-cup gaps, block exposed water |
| Tip burn | Heat stress, high EC, poor calcium movement, low airflow | Lower EC, improve airflow, harvest earlier, reduce heat |
| Bolting or bitter leaves | Hot weather or long crop cycle | Use shade cloth, grow heat-tolerant varieties, harvest younger, plant in cooler seasons |
| pH keeps rising | High alkalinity source water | Test alkalinity, adjust more often, use hard-water nutrients |
| Wilting despite water | High EC or root disease | Check EC, dilute if high, inspect roots |
| Pest damage | Outdoor caterpillars, aphids, whiteflies | Use insect netting, inspect leaves, remove pests early |

## Practical Verdict

The best first build for food production is a small shaded and aerated food-contact DWC system, preferably 5-gallon HDPE bucket modules or a food-service PP/HDPE reservoir. It should be simple, cool, dark, and easy to clean. Do not scale up until the first system proves that water temperature, pH, EC, sanitation, and pests are manageable in the specific greenhouse location.
